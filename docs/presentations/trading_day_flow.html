Trading Day Flow

What the agent does from the opening window to the close. About every 75 seconds a decision cycle manages open positions, rates the whole market, then plans each ticker: a credit spread when premium pays, a debit spread or calendar when it doesn't. The options Wheel runs alongside, staged by you and managed by the same cycle.

Each cycle runs about 15 seconds, then the supervisor waits 60 seconds and starts the next. Outside the window it sleeps until the next trading day, checking the wall clock every 5 minutes so a sleeping laptop can't make it miss the open. The first cycle of each day also settles any Wheel options that expired.

Inside one decision cycle

Stage 1

Manage what's already open

1
Settle expired Wheel options (once a day)

Checks share holdings and records each expired put or call as assigned, called away, or expired worthless.

2
Load positions and value them

Legs are priced at mid, and also at what it would cost to close right now (buy at the ask, sell at the bid). Wheel legs also get their live delta from the option chain.

3
Check exit rules and close

Hard stops, strike-proximity and expiry-eve exits act immediately; other signals must repeat 3 cycles in a row (about 4 minutes). All legs close in one limit order, first between mid and the worst price, then at the worst price. Realized P&L comes from the actual fill.

Stage 2 · step 5

Market risk state

One state for the whole market each cycle. Thresholds were checked against SPY and VIX from 2019 to 2026: in Normal, SPY fell 5% or more within 30 days 8.5% of the time; in Defensive, 31%.

Leaving Caution for Normal needs every condition to clear by a margin, so the state doesn't flip back and forth when VIX hovers near 20. Exits are never gated by the state.

Credit spreads: the scanner tries every combination of expiry (7–30 days), short-strike delta and wing width, and keeps the best one with positive expected value and at least 55% probability of profit. Debit spreads and calendars don't need model edge, since the trend or range is the bet. They must cost no more than 5% over the market mid and pay at least as much as they risk. All prices assume fills at the worse side of the quote, which is what the paper account actually gets.

The options Wheel

Sell a put on a stock you'd be glad to own. If it expires worthless you keep the premium and start again; if you're assigned, you own the shares and sell covered calls until they're called away. New puts pause while the market state is Caution, Defensive or Capitulation.

Expired options are settled by the first cycle of the next trading day. A covered call is never sent unless 100 shares are held.

Paper account, about $29,600. Max risk 2% per trade and 10% of equity across all open spreads; up to 2 positions per ticker with expiries at least 7 days apart, 2 per sector. A single cash-secured put may use up to 40% of equity. Figures reflect the agent's configuration on 5 October 2026.
